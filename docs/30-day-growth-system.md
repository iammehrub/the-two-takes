# Two Takes — 30-Day Operating System

## Positioning

**Real English for Real-Life Situations.** Two Takes helps A2-B1 learners respond naturally in everyday conversations and understand practical spoken English.

This is not a generic English podcast. Each upload must contain one recognizable situation, one specific learner problem, and one usable takeaway.

## Weekly publishing cadence (Asia/Dhaka)

| Day | Format | Time |
|---|---|---:|
| Monday | Short | 10:30 |
| Tuesday | Long video | 08:17 |
| Wednesday | Short | 10:30 |
| Thursday | Long video | 08:17 |
| Friday | Short | 10:30 |
| Saturday | Long video | 08:17 |
| Sunday | Short | 10:30 |

Long videos are published every two days within their own series of slots (Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday). Shorts are published every other day (Monday, Wednesday, Friday, Sunday). This produces 3 long videos and 4 Shorts in a normal seven-day week. A channel upload occurs every day, but each format follows its own spacing.

The Shorts allocation from each long-video production run is:
- Tuesday episode: Wednesday and Friday Shorts.
- Thursday episode: Sunday Short.
- Saturday episode: following Monday Short.

The scheduler publishes only the allocated number of Shorts. A missing or invalid Short is skipped and reported; it is never replaced with an unreviewed file.

## Content pillars

1. **Real-Life Speaking — 40%:** clarification, awkward pauses, polite disagreement, phone calls, requests, interviews, everyday problems.
2. **Listening Challenges — 30%:** short conversations with a question viewers must answer, followed by explanation or replay.
3. **Natural English — 20%:** natural replies, expressions in context, common learner mistakes, conversational transitions.
4. **Story-Based Learning — 10%:** short relatable stories used to teach listening and retelling.

These percentages are editorial priorities, not claims about proven channel performance. Reallocate only after comparing actual results.

## Repeatable series

- **Real Talk:** Himel and Niha role-play one realistic situation and model useful responses.
- **Listen First, Answer Next:** viewers listen before seeing the explanation and answer a comprehension question.
- **What Would You Say?:** a compact response challenge, especially for Shorts.

## Long-video format

Target: approximately 8–10 minutes, with no padding.

- 0:00–0:15 — open on the problem and state the learner payoff.
- 0:15–2:00 — play the situation as a natural conversation.
- 2:00–6:00 — explain 3–5 useful phrases in context.
- 6:00–9:00 — replay, repeat, shadow, or role-play.
- Final minute — one exact practice task and one comment question.

Himel and Niha remain in the single wide two-host composition. Do not use host close-ups or alternate camera shots. Captions must be accurate, speaker-positioned, readable, and emphasize the actual target phrases.

## Shorts format

Target: 18–35 seconds for the current renderer.

Every Short needs:
- A recognizable first-second problem or question.
- One learning objective only.
- A useful example or response.
- Accurate captions and a clear final challenge.
- A title that describes the exact moment, not a generic series name.

Shorts should be independently useful. Do not publish a weak crop merely to hit the weekly count.

## Editorial acceptance checklist

Reject or rewrite an idea if it:
- Could describe hundreds of unrelated English lessons.
- Has no clear real-life situation.
- Teaches no immediately usable response or listening skill.
- Repeats a recent upload's promise.
- Requires a misleading title to sound interesting.

Prefer specific situations with emotional recognition: not understanding someone, going blank mid-conversation, disagreeing politely, making a phone call, correcting a wrong order, or following a fast conversation.

## Analytics review loop

Review each upload at 24 hours, 72 hours, and 7 days.

Long-form:
- Impressions and impressions CTR.
- First 30-second retention when available.
- Average view duration and average percentage viewed.
- Subscribers gained.

Shorts:
- Shorts-feed impressions.
- Stayed-to-watch versus swiped-away.
- Average percentage viewed.
- Subscribers gained.

Keep Shorts and long-form in separate comparisons. Use impressions to interpret CTR, views to interpret watch-time metrics, and sample size before drawing conclusions. Do not treat one successful upload as proof that an entire pillar wins.

## Decision rules

- Impressions but weak CTR: test a clearer title/thumbnail promise.
- Weak opening retention: rewrite the first 15 seconds and show the situation immediately.
- Strong opening but weak overall retention: shorten explanations, add a new example, or move the practice earlier.
- Strong retention but low reach: test adjacent topics and clearer packaging before abandoning the format.
- Subscriber gains: prioritize topics that repeatedly convert viewers, not one-off view spikes.

## 30-day operating targets

- 12–13 long videos.
- 17–18 Shorts.
- 8 community posts, only if the channel has the feature enabled and a useful question to ask.
- One compilation assembled only from coherent, already-approved material.

These are production targets, not guaranteed view or subscriber outcomes.

## Publishing and safety boundaries

- Production code changes do not trigger public uploads.
- Scheduled publishing runs remain limited to the weekly slots above.
- The automated dialogue and media checks are filters, not proof that an episode is good; listen to the final export before relying on it.
- Do not mass-hide or delete older videos based only on low views. Review the verified inventory individually.
- No purchased views, artificial engagement, misleading metadata, or copied scripts/branding.
- Missing analytics stay marked unavailable; never invent CTR, retention, or audience demand.
